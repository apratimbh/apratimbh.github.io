Abstract

Vision-language models have shown impressive progress in recent years. However, existing models are largely limited to turn-based interactions, where each turn must be stepped (i.e., prompted) by the user. Open-ended, asynchronous interactions, where an AI model may proactively deliver timely responses or feedback based on the unfolding situation in real-time, are an open challenge. In this work, we present the QEVD benchmark and dataset, which explores human-AI interaction in the challenging, yet controlled, real-world domain of fitness coaching—a task which intrinsically requires monitoring live user activity and providing immediate feedback. The benchmark requires vision-language models to recognize complex human actions, identify possible mistakes, and provide appropriate feedback in real-time. Our experiments reveal the limitations of existing state-of-the-art vision-language models for such asynchronous situated interactions. Motivated by this, we propose a simple end-to-end streaming baseline that can respond asynchronously to human actions with appropriate feedback at the appropriate time.

What to Say, and When to Say It

Proactive Mistake Corrections

A live coach must recognize a mistake as it becomes visible, give a useful correction, and acknowledge the participant’s response. FIT-COACH captures these exchanges: asking for a deeper squat, correcting hand placement in a push-up, or coordinating arms and legs during jumping jacks.

“Yes! That's it! Now try to make your squats deeper!”

“Nice deep squat! Keep it up!”

Multi-hop Feedback Across Time

Later feedback can depend on earlier corrections, completed repetitions, and previous exercises. A coach needs to remember what was said and connect it to the participant’s evolving movement. The teaser shows corrections carried into repetition counting, and a reminder about squat depth that refers back to an earlier exercise.

“I like your stance! Now, try to squat down lower!”

“10 reps! Keep going and try to squat down lower!!”

Dataset and Benchmark

The Qualcomm Exercise Video Dataset (QEVD) combines short clips for fine-grained fitness understanding with longer workout sessions for interactive coaching. Annotations cover exercise recognition, questions and answers, corrective feedback, encouragement, and repetition counting.

The full FIT-COACH collection contains 364 workout videos spanning 21.6 hours of recorded activity. The 290-video extended training set combines the original 149 training videos, 100 former CVPR 2025 competition videos, and 41 additional workouts. Durations are measured from the original recording timestamps.

The original 74-video FIT-COACH benchmark remains the test set. Each session contains five to six exercises, with feedback aligned to visible actions. Evaluation asks both when the model should intervene and what it should say.

The paper also introduces Stream-VLM, an end-to-end streaming baseline that processes incoming video and decides when to provide feedback. The benchmark exposes the challenges of combining fine-grained perception, memory, and timely communication.
